## D1 — The composition follows the record, never a flag

`PageHero` has one photographic composition and one band, and **the presence of `media` is the whole condition**.

- **With a picture:** the hero takes `100svh` minus the header, the photograph is full-bleed under the measured 64 → 74% scrim, the mesh and the track lines are not painted, and white is the one text tier.
- **Without one:** ADR-0098 D2's ink band, unchanged in every respect.
- **No `variant` prop, no `hasImage` boolean, no `height` enum.** Each of those is a second source of truth for a fact the data already carries, and each is wrong the moment an editor uploads a picture.

The visible breadcrumb is a separate question and keeps its separate answer: a page draws the trail it passes. The `/about` pages pass none (ADR-0072 D7) and the listing pages pass theirs (IA §8.5, mandatory from depth two). The ink hero drew one unconditionally, which reversed D7 in practice; passing nothing reinstates it.

## D2 — The institutional heroes keep their own composition, and unification is deferred

`IdentityHero` (`apps/web/src/components/ui/identity-hero.tsx`) stays the photographic hero of `/about/president`, `/about/governance/vision-mission` and `/about/governance/strategic-plan`. The kit's `PageHero` is the photographic hero of every listing page.

There are in fact **four** hero implementations in the tree, and only two are live: `IdentityHero` and the kit's `PageHero` render pages; `apps/web/src/components/ui/page-hero.tsx` renders none and is kept only because `surface-standard.spec.ts:134-137` requires the file to exist, which is a guard pinning dead code rather than guarding live code; `contact-hero.tsx` serves `/contact` alone and was never migrated. Pointing that guard at the live heroes and letting the dead one go is cheap and independent of unification — it is listed in the discharge plan below rather than done here, because it changes a guard on a pass that is restoring a defect.

This is **two components for one idea, knowingly**, and the reason is that the alternative trades a real defect for a tidiness goal on the same pass. `IdentityHero` carries measured geometry that nothing else on the site reproduces: the identity-line groups at the ascent angle, the portrait's column track, and the title's reserve above group A. Re-expressing that in the kit is not a move, it is a re-derivation, and the acceptance criterion the Product Owner set is that the restored pages match their "before" state exactly.

**Discharge plan**, for the session that takes it, with the cost stated so it is chosen rather than stumbled into:

1. Move the pure-geometry parts (`GROUP_A`/`GROUP_B`, `LineStroke`, `GroupA`/`GroupB`, `PhotoLines`, `SeamLines`) into `packages/brand-ui/accent/identity-lines.tsx`. They have no application dependency; the arithmetic is tokens and numbers.
2. Move `.pm-settle`, `.pm-ground`, `.pm-portrait` and `.il-stroke` into the kit's stylesheet. `motion-contract.spec.ts` asserts `--motion-duration-ambient` has exactly one consumer named `motion.css: .pm-ground`; that assertion moves with the rule or the guard goes vacuous.
3. Add the kit's file to `seo-contract.spec.ts`'s `<h1>` allowlist and `surface-standard.spec.ts`'s hero list, or extend both walkers to `packages/brand-ui` — **not** drop the entries, which is how a guard is weakened by a refactor.
4. Keep `SeamLines` reachable: the homepage's sponsor sections use it, and the homepage is out of scope for hero work.
5. Separately and cheaply, whether or not the rest is done: point `surface-standard.spec.ts`'s hero list at the heroes that actually render, and delete `ui/page-hero.tsx`. Today that list is the only reason the dead file survives.

Until that is done, `identity-hero.tsx`'s own doc comment is the record of which component is which.

## D3 — The entrance is CSS, plays once, and animates `transform` and `opacity` only

No JavaScript, no dependency, no scroll coupling. Every rule sits inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` and inside `:root:not([data-motion-off~="hero"])`, so a reader who has asked for less motion, or a deployment with `UAEAF_MOTION_OFF=hero` set, gets the finished hero in its first frame with no animation to zero out.

The sequence, all of it composited:

| Element | Move | Starts | Runs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The photograph | `scale(1.04) → 1`, opaque throughout | 0 | 1200ms |
| The exposure plane | `opacity: 0.45 → 0` — a black plane clearing, which is `brightness(0.55) → 1` composited | 0 | 1200ms |
| The breadcrumb | `translate` along the 45° ascent, `opacity: 0.01 → 1` | 3 steps | 640ms |
| Heading, then subtitle | over a photograph, masked: a clip box with an inner `translateY(105%) → 0`. On the band, the same rise as the breadcrumb | 4 and 5 steps | 640ms |
| The streaks | `scaleY(0) → 1` from their own base | 4 steps | 640ms |

A step is `--motion-ascent-stagger` (60ms). The last line starts at five steps — **300ms, inside §5.7's 600ms total-stagger ceiling**. The scrim does not animate: at frame 0 the exposure plane already composites darker than the scrim's resting value, so easing the scrim up from 82% would have made the opening *lighter* under a layer that is darkening it — and holding it at its measured value means the words never stand on less wash than they were measured against. Every animation fills `backwards` rather than `both`: each keyframe's end state is already the element's resting value, so a forwards fill would add nothing to the finished frame and only keep the effect, and its compositor layer, in force after it ended.

**The mask applies only to a line that has a clip box.** `translateY(105%)` on an element with nothing clipping it is not a reveal — it starts a line below where it belongs and slides up over whatever is beneath it, which is what the breadcrumb did until it was measured. The breadcrumb therefore rises in both compositions and is never masked. The two durations are component-local CSS variables, not tokens, and the reason is the same one ADR-0099 D3 gave: the longest duration token is `--motion-duration-ambient` at 1200ms and `motion-contract.spec.ts` binds it to one selector under ADR-0069 D9. A second consumer of that token is a governance change; a local variable with its derivation beside it is not. **The scale stays at eight values.**

Three constraints are structural rather than promised:

- **Every line is `display: block`.** A transform does not apply to a non-replaced inline box. The first implementation left the line an inline `<span>`, so the browser attached the animation, reported it as running, and moved nothing: measured on `/ar/news`, the heading's computed transform was the identity matrix at frame 0 while `getAnimations()` named `brand-hero-unmask`. The mask and the rise were both silently absent and the hero looked finished. Nothing else in the suite would have caught it — the keyframes were valid, the guard wrapper was correct, and the animations existed.


- **The photograph never animates `opacity`.** An element at `opacity: 0` is not counted as painted, so fading in a hero photograph postpones the Largest Contentful Paint by the length of the fade for a decorative gain. Only its scale moves, and a contract spec fails if `opacity` appears in that keyframe.
- **The mask is scoped to the photographic composition.** On the band the heading *is* the largest paint, and a clip box delays it exactly as `opacity: 0` would. A masking rule outside that scope fails the same spec.

Measured on the live page, 390×844, after the entrance: **CLS 0 with no shift entries at all** on `/about/president` and `/about/governance/vision-mission`, in both languages, with the Largest Contentful Paint on the `<img>`.

## D4 — The kit's stylesheets are guarded by nothing, and this one is

`apps/web/src/lib/design-system/page-hero-photo-contract.spec.ts` asserts, against the file's text: the keyframes animate `transform`/`opacity` only; no animation runs outside a `no-preference` guard; the off switch is honoured; the mask never escapes the photographic composition; the ground has no `opacity` to fade from; a text line never starts at exactly zero opacity; and both scrim stops clear AA over a pure white photograph and never fall below `COVER_SCRIM_MIN`.

It is **proven by mutation**, not trusted: a forbidden property in a keyframe, a scrim stop dropped to 40%, and a mask rule unscoped each turn it red — five of its twelve tests failed together when all three were applied, and the file was restored.

**The general gap stays open and is recorded here rather than papered over.** `motion-contract.spec.ts` walks `apps/web/src` and `packages/design-tokens/css`; `token-contract.spec.ts` walks `apps/web/src`; `source-files.ts` adds `packages/brand-ui` but only its `.tsx`. So no keyframe whitelist, duration-token rule or hex rule reaches any of the kit's four stylesheets. Extending the existing walkers is the right fix and is **not** done here, because `accent/accent.css` already animates a custom property those walkers would reject, and turning a green suite red on files this change does not touch is how a guard gets disabled instead of fixed.
